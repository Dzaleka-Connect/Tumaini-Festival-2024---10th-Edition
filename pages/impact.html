Economic & Social Impact

What the festival brings to Dzaleka: income, work, and a public stage.

$150K+ Annual community revenue
2,335 Entrepreneurs supported
1,500 Seasonal jobs
2,000+ Homestay beneficiaries
400 Women trained each year
61M+ Media reach

Data note: Figures are as reported by Tumaini Letu, the festival organizer.

Livelihoods

The festival brings visitors, and their spending, into Dzaleka. Organizers report more than $150,000 in community revenue each year.

  • Vendors: 2,335 food, craft, and clothing sellers supported since 2014.
  • Homestays: visitors stay with around 400 host families, benefiting roughly 2,000 people.
  • Local work: about 1,500 seasonal jobs in building, sound, stage, security, and catering.

A public stage

Camp-based musicians, dancers, poets, and actors share the bill with national and international artists. For three days the camp is a venue, and its residents are the hosts.

Refugees and hosts together

The festival is free and open to the Malawian host community as well as camp residents. It gives the two a shared space, and gives outside audiences a view of Dzaleka beyond the usual headlines.
